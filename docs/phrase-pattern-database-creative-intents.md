# Phrase Pattern Database Creative Intents

## Purpose

This document groups the existing internal structural template families into higher-level **Creative Intent Categories**. The template families describe reusable phrase mechanics; the creative intents explain **why a person would emotionally connect with, buy, gift, or wear** a phrase-based product made from those mechanics.

The taxonomy is intentionally niche-agnostic. It does not add new phrase patterns and does not create fantasy-specific or niche-specific systems. Instead, it summarizes the recurring psychological, emotional, social, and commercial jobs served by the current template families in `data/phrase-patterns/internal-structural-template-families.jsonl`.

## Method

The current template families were analyzed as product-design assets rather than as isolated phrases. Each family was reviewed for:

- the social signal it lets a buyer send;
- the emotional payoff it creates;
- the visual treatments it naturally supports;
- its ability to support humor, identity, gifting, and repeat purchases;
- its usefulness across many unrelated niches.

Because one template family can serve more than one human motivation, the mapping is intentionally many-to-many. For example, `Faux Certification` can support identity, competence, humor, and authority play; `Preference Statement` can support priority, escape, boundaries, and lifestyle aspiration.

## Scoring Definitions

Scores use a 1-10 product-design scale:

- **Humor Potential**: likelihood that the category can generate funny, relatable, or self-aware products.
- **Identity Potential**: strength as a wearable self-label or social signal.
- **Visual Potential**: ease of turning the intent into badges, signs, icons, layouts, souvenir treatments, or other product-ready compositions.
- **Commercial Potential**: likely usefulness for POD products, gifting, evergreen niches, and repeatable listing concepts.
- **Cross-Niche Applicability**: ability to work across hundreds of niches without depending on a single fandom, brand, or subculture.

## Creative Intent Categories

### Identity and Role Claim

**Description:** Turns a product into a wearable label: a concise declaration of who the buyer is, what role they occupy, or what identity they want others to recognize.

**Human motivation it serves:** self-expression, recognition, social signaling, pride, personal branding.

**Typical emotions:** pride, confidence, affection, belonging, playful seriousness.

**Typical use cases:**

- self-purchase by people who want a clear identity label
- gifts that affirm a role or personality
- club, group, team, workplace, or family-role products
- profile-style apparel, stickers, mugs, tote bags, and badges

**Related template families:** Identity Declaration, Checklist Identity, Better With Companion, Problem Solver Role, Faux Certification.

**Example niches:** professions, hobbies, pets, family roles, fitness, gaming, gardening, coffee.

**Scores:**

- Humor Potential: 7/10
- Identity Potential: 10/10
- Visual Potential: 8/10
- Commercial Potential: 10/10
- Cross-Niche Applicability: 10/10

## Cross-Family Observations

### 1. The strongest commercial axis is identity plus humor

The most product-ready families usually let the buyer both identify themselves and laugh at themselves. `Warning Label`, `Faux Certification`, `Identity Declaration`, `Mood Forecast`, `Collection Habit`, and `Cause And Effect Excuse` are especially strong because they create a public-facing persona rather than merely describing a topic.

### 2. Visual formats are part of the motivation

Many families imply a visual object: labels, warnings, badges, seals, checklists, forecasts, rule cards, souvenir badges, and maps. This matters commercially because phrase-based products perform better when the phrase suggests an immediate layout rather than requiring a designer to invent one from scratch.

### 3. Buyers often purchase permission

Several families make an indulgence, obsession, boundary, or imperfection feel acceptable. `Collection Habit` gives permission to accumulate; `Preference Statement` gives permission to prefer leisure over duty; `Boundary Statement` gives permission to be unavailable; `Skill In Progress` gives permission to be imperfect.

### 4. Giftability comes from recognizability

A phrase-based product is easier to gift when the giver can recognize the recipient in the phrase. The highest-gift categories are Identity and Role Claim, Comic Self-Portrait, Competence and Authority Play, Enthusiast Excess and Collection, and Ritual, Routine, and Fuel.

### 5. Categories should remain motivations, not niches

These creative intents should not be used as niche folders. A gardening design, for example, could be Identity and Role Claim, Ritual, Escape and Happy Place, Values and Craft Ethos, Growth, Collection, or Comic Self-Portrait depending on the buyer motivation.

## Recommended Use In FusionCanvas

Use Creative Intent Categories as an intermediate layer between a user's niche and the template-family database:

1. Identify the user's niche or product audience.
2. Choose the buyer motivation that best fits the desired product angle.
3. Retrieve related template families.
4. Generate niche-specific adaptations from those families.
5. Score or filter concepts by humor, identity, visual potential, commercial potential, and cross-niche applicability.

This keeps the system focused on the fundamental reasons people connect with phrase-based products instead of treating phrase generation as a purely linguistic substitution task.
